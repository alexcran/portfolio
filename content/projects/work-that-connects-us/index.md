A feature article written for Maola's farmer-facing *Pipeline* magazine, looking back on June Dairy Month across the cooperative. It gathers the month's activities, which were led by Communications and Marketing, and uses them to trace the long journey of a gallon of milk and the many people, in very different roles and separated by hundreds of miles, who move it from farm to family.

*Published in the Maola Pipeline Report (Spring 2026). Written for Maola's internal publication.*

## Context

The brief was a recap of a month of internal activities — the kind of thing that usually reads as a list of events with photos. The harder, better version treats those activities as evidence of something: that a cooperative is the *connection* between farms, plants, labs, routes, and offices, not any one of them. Written in Maola's institutional first-person-plural voice for our farmers.

## My Role

I wrote and structured the article as well as acted as the dairy month coordinator. The design and layout of the printed spread were done by Agency29, Maola's agency of record.

---

June arrives quietly in a dairy cooperative, in the steady rhythm of tankers pulling onto farms before sunrise, production lines beginning to hum awake, and drivers heading onto dark roads while most communities are still asleep. Milk moves from one set of hands to another across our cooperative long before most people begin their day, the same way it does every morning of the year.

Last June, though, across our plants, branches, offices, and facilities, people paused long enough to look more closely at the work behind that movement and the people connected to it.

At several locations, folding tables slowly filled with handwritten cards addressed to our farmer-owners. Employees stopped to write between meetings, production runs, routes, and shift changes, adding messages beside coffee cups, safety glasses, and radios crackling in the background.

The messages were heartfelt, occasionally uneven, and unmistakably sincere. Together, they reflected something easy to lose sight of in the pace of daily operations: every carton, jug, half-pint, and tanker load moving through our cooperative begins somewhere long before it reaches a plant floor, grocery shelf, school cafeteria, or family refrigerator.

It begins on one of our farms, often in the dark hours before dawn, when milking parlors glow against the morning sky and another day's work is already underway.

That understanding quietly shaped the month.

Across June, conversations moved from our farms to the customers and communities our cooperative serves, from food safety to the employees helping carry dairy into homes, schools, restaurants, and neighborhoods across the region every day. On paper, the month was organized around weekly themes. In practice, it became a way of tracing the long journey of milk through the cooperative itself and recognizing the number of people required to move it carefully from beginning to end.

Photos and stories from member farms appeared across digital signage, emails, posters, and the intranet, placing faces and families alongside the products employees work with every day. At some locations, employees gathered around breakroom tables answering dairy trivia questions between shifts while handwritten thank-you cards continued piling up nearby.

"I have learned and I have seen the importance of our farmers," one employee wrote. "The performance you provide is outstanding."

The message was simple, but it captured something true about the relationship between farmers and employees inside a cooperative: one depends on the other.

Every gallon of milk entrusted to our cooperative passes through dozens of hands before reaching a customer, and every one of those hands carries responsibility for the people who produced it. Last June gave many employees a clearer view of where their work fits within that journey.

As the month continued, employees across our cooperative explored where our products go after leaving our facilities: schools, grocery stores, restaurants, foodservice operations, ingredient customers, and homes across the region. Maps appeared on walls. Product graphics circulated through facilities. Conversations during pre-shift meetings turned toward the scale and reach of dairy moving through our network each day.

At several locations, celebrations included products tied to local customers, small reminders that the work happening inside our plants and branches continues far beyond the dock doors.

Milk may begin on our farms, but bringing it safely into communities depends on thousands of decisions made quietly every day across our cooperative. A driver backing into a school dock before sunrise. A laboratory technician checking a result for the second time. A production employee watching fill levels move down a line. A sanitation crew finishing equipment cleanup long after the end of a shift.

Much of the work is repetitive. Much of it happens out of sight. All of it matters.

"Quality starts with you," one employee had written earlier in the month.

It was a note to our farmers, but it also described the cooperative itself. Quality begins on our farms and continues through every employee who handles, tests, processes, packages, transports, or delivers dairy afterward. The work changes from role to role, but the responsibility remains shared.

That became especially clear during the month's focus on food safety culture.

Across our facilities, food safety reminders appeared on digital screens, in inboxes, and taped beside timeclocks and production areas. Employees revisited the everyday habits that protect product quality and consumer trust through quizzes, shift-change conversations, and short discussions led by operations and quality teams.

Behind those reminders was something larger than compliance.

Families trust the milk carrying our name. Customers trust the products arriving at their stores and facilities. And our farmers trust every employee throughout the cooperative to protect the quality of the milk they worked to produce.

By the final weeks of June, attention turned toward the employees carrying that responsibility every day. Across many locations, teams gathered for lunches, ice cream socials, sundae bars, giveaways, and appreciation events. Team photos appeared on digital displays throughout the cooperative while coworkers shared meals together between shifts and routes. In some locations, drivers received appreciation polos before heading back out onto the road.

Individually, many of the moments were small. A handwritten card curling slightly at the edges after sitting in a breakroom for days. A conversation during shift change.

Together, though, those moments revealed something important about our cooperative.

A dairy cooperative is not only farms, nor is it only plants, branches, labs, warehouses, offices, or delivery routes. It is the connection between all those things. It is the early mornings on our farms and the production lines running through the night. It is the driver unloading milk at a school before sunrise and the employee sanitizing equipment long after production ends. It is people in very different roles, often separated by hundreds of miles, depending on one another every single day.

Last June, employees across our cooperative spent a month celebrating dairy. In the process, many also gained a clearer view of the people and purpose behind the work they do every day.

Now, as another June Dairy Month begins once again across our cooperative, that work continues in thousands of steady moments, from the farms where the milk begins to the employees helping carry it into communities across the region.
